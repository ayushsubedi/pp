Flagged risk: direct earnest-money custody in 2–3 states inside 6 months is a state-licensing problem, not an engineering one — broker trust accounts / escrow licenses are typically per-state and slow to obtain properly. Recommendation: partner with a licensed escrow/title company for Phase 1 (RealEZ still looks and feels like it's tracking the money); treat true self-custody as a Phase 2+ goal.

Why this team differs from B

AI coding assistance amplifies whoever directs it. A senior engineer with Claude ships fast and correct; a junior engineer with Claude ships fast and confidently wrong — dangerous specifically where the product touches real legal/financial logic. So the team gets more senior for feature work, but two things move in the other direction: DevOps/Platform becomes a required hire (not optional, since the founder isn't hands-on and foundations need an explicit owner), and QA/Verification starts in month 1 instead of month 2, because AI-generated code needs adversarial checking from day one. Full reasoning: Team.

Team (6 new hires)

You (non-coding) + 1 FTE backend dev + 1 FTE Flutter dev, plus:

  1. Founding/Staff Engineer (de facto tech lead, primary reviewer of AI-generated code)
  2. DevOps/Platform Engineer (required, not optional — owns foundations from week 1)
  3. Second Mobile/Flutter Engineer
  4. QA/Verification Engineer (starts alongside foundations, not after)
  5. Product/UX Designer
  6. Project Manager (joins month 2, once there's real coordination complexity)

Timeline shape

Month 1: Foundations (CI/CD, IaC, security baseline) run in parallel with pillar design, owned by the Founding Engineer + DevOps hires from day one. Months 2–3 Core Build. Months 4–5 Test & Polish. See Gantt.

Exit criteria

  • Everything in Scenario B's exit criteria, plus:
  • CI pipeline enforcing lint, type-check, tests, and security scanning on every merge
  • Infrastructure defined as code, not hand-configured
  • A written, followed AI-usage policy distinguishing AI-generate-freely from always-human-authored code

Top risks

  • Hiring two strong technical co-leads (Founding Engineer + DevOps) fast enough to start month 1 on foundations, not features
  • Review discipline eroding under deadline pressure — the whole model depends on HITL review actually happening on every merge
  • E-sig vendor contracting delay (same as B)
  • Mobile parity beyond Agent/Client sliding into Phase 2 (same as B)

All three scenarios assume GTM and Compliance are owned by other cofounders, staffed and budgeted separately from this build plan.
