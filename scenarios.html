Scenarios

Three full plans for the same six-ish months, compared on the same structure so they're actually comparable. Jump straight to one, or read all three.

Objective

Live pilot with 1–2 real brokerages across 2–3 states by month 6: real agents, real clients, real transactions, real legal and financial stakes.

Key inputs

E-signatureBuild in-house, ESIGN/UETA-compliant
MobileFull native (iOS + Android) parity with web
Pilot geography2–3 states
Money handlingRealEZ holds/moves earnest money directly
Timeline6 months
Flagged risk: direct earnest-money custody in 2–3 states inside 6 months is a state-licensing problem, not an engineering one — broker trust accounts / escrow licenses are typically per-state and slow to obtain properly. Recommendation: partner with a licensed escrow/title company for Phase 1 (RealEZ still looks and feels like it's tracking the money); treat true self-custody as a Phase 2+ goal.

Team (5 new hires)

You (architect/AWS/backend/CTO, hands-on) + 1 FTE backend dev + 1 FTE Flutter dev, plus:

  1. Senior Backend Engineer #2
  2. Compliance & Trust Operations Lead
  3. Product/UX Designer
  4. QA/Automation Engineer
  5. HR/Payroll/Admin

No dedicated GTM hire — pilot brokerage sourcing is owned by other cofounders in every scenario, including this one, kept consistent across the board. No second mobile engineer either: one senior Flutter engineer with AI-assisted development is the default bet everywhere on this site (see Team).

Timeline shape

Months 1–2 Foundation · Months 3–4 Build · Months 5–6 Harden & Launch. See Gantt.

Exit criteria

  • 10–20 real transactions closed end-to-end across the pilot brokerages
  • E-signature engine reviewed and approved by outside counsel, no open exceptions
  • Escrow-partner trust flow running with zero reconciliation discrepancies
  • Native iOS + Android apps live for Agent and Client at minimum
  • Jurisdiction rules validated for all pilot states by counsel
  • Pen test complete, critical/high findings remediated

Top risks

  • Money-transmitter/escrow licensing arriving late or being shortcut
  • Full native parity across 5 portals with one Flutter engineer
  • Legal review as a bottleneck across e-sig, escrow, and 2–3 state pilot agreements
  • Founder bandwidth split across architecture, AWS, backend, and leadership

Why this differs from B

It hires the exact same five roles B does, at the exact same cost. The difference is entirely sequencing and discipline: QA starts in month 1 instead of month 2, because AI-generated code needs adversarial checking from day one, not a testing pass bolted on later. And the engineering-foundations checklist is a required deliverable in week 1 — jointly owned by the founder and the Senior Backend Engineer #2 — rather than something that happens if there's time once feature work is underway. Full reasoning: Team.

Team (5 new hires — identical to B)

You (non-coding) + 1 FTE backend dev + 1 FTE Flutter dev, plus:

  1. Senior Backend Engineer #2 (co-owns architecture, sets up foundations in week 1)
  2. HR/Payroll/Admin (in early, alongside #1, to onboard everyone else joining)
  3. QA/Verification Engineer (starts alongside foundations, month 1, not month 2)
  4. Product/UX Designer
  5. Project Manager (joins month 2, once there's real coordination complexity)

Timeline shape

Month 1: Foundations (CI/CD, IaC, security baseline) run in parallel with pillar design, owned by the Senior Backend Engineer #2 and the founder from day one — no dedicated platform hire. Months 2–3 Core Build. Months 4–5 Test & Polish. See Gantt.

Exit criteria

  • Everything in Scenario B's exit criteria, plus:
  • CI pipeline enforcing lint, type-check, tests, and security scanning on every merge
  • Infrastructure defined as code, not hand-configured
  • A written, followed AI-usage policy distinguishing AI-generate-freely from always-human-authored code

Top risks

  • Foundations work competing for the Senior Backend Engineer #2's time against actual feature deadlines, with no dedicated platform hire to absorb it
  • Review discipline eroding under deadline pressure — the whole model depends on HITL review actually happening on every merge
  • E-sig vendor contracting delay (same as B)
  • Agent + Client native parity resting on one senior Flutter engineer, not two (same as B)

GTM (pilot brokerage sourcing) is owned by other cofounders in all three scenarios. Compliance is staffed internally in Scenario A (a Compliance & Trust Ops Lead) and owned externally in B and C.
