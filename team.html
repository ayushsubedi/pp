Who do we actually need

Team

The question isn't "how many people" — it's what actually creates risk in this specific build, and who removes that risk fastest. With room for 8–10 people at no additional cost, the two roles cut in the leaner pass come back: they were cut for budget discipline, not because the work wasn't real.

The one idea underneath all of this: AI coding assistance means a senior team covers more ground than the same size team could before. That's what made a lean team viable at all. It doesn't mean a dedicated platform owner or a second mobile engineer stop being real risk-reduction — it means a lean team could survive without them. Given the budget headroom, there's no reason to keep carrying that risk.

Already on the team

  • You Architect / CTO — hands-on in Scenario A, non-coding (team, vision, roadmap, architecture) in B and C
  • FTE Backend Engineer — the developer already building today
  • FTE Flutter Engineer — the developer already building today

Hiring plan — tiered, same across A, B, and C

Tier 1 · Week 1

  • 1
    Land first · FTE

    Senior Backend Engineer #2 — the tech lead

    Not a second peer to the existing Backend Engineer — this is the tech lead. Co-owns architecture with the founder, is the primary reviewer of AI-generated code across the team. The single highest-leverage hire on this list, and the one place seniority is worth a real premium (see Cost).

  • 2
    Land first, alongside #1 · FTE

    DevOps / Platform Engineer

    Back in the plan given the headroom. Owns CI/CD, infrastructure-as-code, secrets, and the security baseline as an actual job, not something the tech lead absorbs on top of feature work — freeing the tech lead to spend week 1 on architecture and review setup instead of splitting attention across both.

  • 3
    Land first, alongside #1 · FTE

    HR / Payroll / Admin

    Brought in early on purpose: this person processes the paperwork, payroll, and onboarding for everyone else joining over the next few weeks.

Tier 2 · Weeks 2–4

  • 4
    Weeks 2–3 · FTE

    Second Mobile / Flutter Engineer

    Back in the plan too. One Flutter engineer covering Agent + Client native parity alone was always flagged as the tightest assumption in the leaner plan — a real risk to accept only because headcount was budget-constrained. It no longer is.

  • 5
    Weeks 2–3 · FTE

    QA / Verification Engineer

    Starts as early as the scenario allows (month 1 in C, month 2 in B/A) — AI-generated code needs adversarial verification from the start. Owns the test pyramid from day one, plus deep adversarial testing on the two pillars and anything money-adjacent.

  • 6
    Weeks 3–4 · Project-based, not FTE

    UI/UX Designer

    One designer, engaged as a project/contract, not a full-time hire — a flat project cost (NPR 4,00,000 total, see Cost), not a monthly salary line. Covers the Transitional Document authoring flow and the Merchant Integration onboarding flow, plus the mobile design system.

Tier 3 · Month 2

  • 7
    Month 2, deliberately not week 1 · FTE

    Project Manager

    One PM, across all three scenarios — earns its keep once there are 6+ builders and real cross-team dependency dates to track.

8 hired, room for up to 2 more

6 new FTE hires (tech lead, DevOps, second mobile, QA, PM, HR/Admin) plus the 2 existing engineers puts the team at 8 — the low end of the 8–10 headroom. The remaining room is real but not pre-committed to a specific role: a second backend engineer if Scenario A's 2–3-state jurisdiction-rules workload turns out to need it, or a security-focused engineer once there's real production traffic to justify it over a contract pen test. Better to leave that headroom open than invent a role to fill a round number.

What's still not hired, and why

  • A Compliance & Trust Operations Lead — even in Scenario A's direct-custody path, compliance is owned by other cofounders, not staffed inside the build team. This isn't a budget call, it's a "not this team's job" call — see the detailed earnest-money custody risk on Scenario A.
  • A dedicated "AI tooling" or prompt-engineering specialist — a practice (a written AI-usage policy, see Tech Stack), not a role, at this team size
  • A dedicated security engineer as an FTE — contract a pen test before pilot launch instead, unless the headroom above gets used here
  • Any GTM hire on the build side — owned by other cofounders in every scenario
