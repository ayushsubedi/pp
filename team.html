Who do we actually need

Team

The question isn't "how many people" — it's what actually creates risk in this specific build, and who removes that risk fastest. Written the way I'd actually staff this if it were my own money and my own pilot.

The one idea underneath all of this: AI coding assistance means a small senior team can safely cover more ground than a same-size team could before — a senior engineer with Claude, using well-reviewed Terraform/CI templates, can stand up solid infrastructure without a dedicated platform hire; one senior Flutter engineer can cover Agent + Client native parity without a second. That's why this team is leaner than a pre-AI plan would be. What doesn't shrink: review discipline and early testing, because AI-generated code still needs a human verifying the parts that touch money and law.

What AI-native building actually changes here

  1. Fewer heads, not more. Raw typing speed stops being the bottleneck the moment everyone has Claude, and each senior person's effective range widens — a senior backend engineer can reasonably own infrastructure setup alongside feature work; a senior mobile engineer can reasonably own both platforms alone. Headcount that would have been needed to cover ground is spent instead on seniority.
  2. Review and verification capacity is still the real constraint. AI-generated code that looks right and quietly isn't is the actual risk, specifically in commission math, e-signature handling, and jurisdiction rules. QA starting in month 1 instead of month 2, and mandatory review on every merge, are what keep a leaner team safe rather than just fast.
  3. Foundations still need to exist before feature work — that doesn't go away just because there's no dedicated platform hire. It becomes a checklist the founder and Senior Backend Engineer #2 are explicitly accountable for in week 1, not a role someone else owns.

Hiring plan — tiered (Scenarios B and C; A adds Compliance instead of PM)

Tier 1 · Week 1

  • 1
    Land first

    Senior Backend Engineer #2

    Co-owns architecture with the founder and is the primary reviewer of AI-generated code across the team. Also stands up CI/CD, infrastructure-as-code, and the security baseline in week 1, using AI-assisted scaffolding rather than a dedicated platform hire — the single highest-leverage hire on this list.

  • 2
    Land first, alongside #1

    HR / Payroll / Admin

    Brought in early on purpose: this person processes the paperwork, payroll, and onboarding for everyone else joining over the next few weeks. Delaying this hire just means the founder or the Senior Backend Engineer does admin instead of their actual job.

Tier 2 · Weeks 2–4

  • 3
    Weeks 2–3

    QA / Verification Engineer

    Starts alongside foundations, not after — AI-generated code needs adversarial verification from the start. Owns the test pyramid from day one and does deep, adversarial testing specifically on the two pillars and anything money-adjacent.

  • 4
    Weeks 3–4

    Product / UX Designer

    The Transitional Document's authoring flow and the Merchant Integration onboarding flow are genuinely hard UX problems, not just forms — worth a dedicated senior designer rather than engineers designing as they build.

Tier 3 · Month 2

  • 5
    Month 2, deliberately not week 1

    Project Manager (B and C)

    Earns its keep once there are 5+ builders and real cross-team dependency dates to track — brought in earlier than that, a PM mostly adds process overhead to a small team more efficient talking directly. In Scenario A, this slot is a Compliance & Trust Operations Lead instead, since A staffs compliance internally.

What I'd explicitly not hire

  • A dedicated DevOps/Platform Engineer — the exact reasoning above: a senior backend engineer with AI-assisted IaC/CI scaffolding covers this at this team size. Revisit once there's real production traffic and on-call load to justify a dedicated owner.
  • A second mobile engineer — one senior Flutter engineer with AI assistance can cover Agent + Client native parity. This is a tighter assumption than a two-person mobile team, and it's the risk most worth watching (see risks on each scenario page), but it's the right default bet.
  • A dedicated "AI tooling" or prompt-engineering specialist — a practice (a written AI-usage policy, see Foundations), not a role, at this team size
  • A dedicated security engineer as an FTE — contract a pen test before pilot launch instead
  • Any GTM hire on the build side — owned by other cofounders in every scenario

Scenario B vs. Scenario C — same team, different discipline

B and C now hire the exact same five roles, at the exact same cost (see Cost). The difference is entirely sequencing and discipline: in C, QA starts in month 1 instead of month 2, and the engineering-foundations checklist (see Foundations) is a required, checked-off deliverable from week 1 — jointly owned by the founder and the Senior Backend Engineer #2 — rather than something that happens if there's time. Same team, same money, more disciplined order of operations.
