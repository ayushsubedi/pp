Who do we actually need

Team

The question isn't "how many people" — it's what actually creates risk in this specific build, and who removes that risk fastest. Written the way I'd actually staff this if it were my own money and my own pilot.

The one idea underneath all of this: AI coding assistance amplifies whoever is directing it. A senior engineer with Claude ships fast and correct. A junior engineer with Claude ships fast and confidently wrong — code that looks right, passes a cursory read, and fails exactly where it's most expensive to fail: commission math, e-signature callback handling, jurisdiction rules, anything that determines who owes whom what. That single fact reshapes almost every hiring call below.

Three things that change once AI is doing the typing

  1. The team gets more senior, not larger, for pure feature work. Raw typing speed stops being the bottleneck the moment everyone has Claude. What doesn't get automated is judgment: does this design hold up, is this edge case handled, would this pass a legal audit. That's a senior skill regardless of who or what wrote the first draft.
  2. Review and verification capacity becomes the real constraint. If code arrives faster than it can be properly reviewed, the team either slows down for review (fine) or starts merging on trust (not fine, and it compounds silently until an incident forces the issue). Hire for review bandwidth on purpose, don't let it become the accidental bottleneck nobody staffed for.
  3. Foundations have to exist before feature work, not alongside it. A team moving fast with AI assistance and no CI gates, no required review, no IaC, is moving fast toward a mess that costs far more to unwind later than it would have cost to prevent in week 1. This is the most common founder mistake in a from-scratch build, AI-assisted or not — and AI-assisted teams hit the wall faster because they generate volume faster.

Scenario C hiring plan — tiered

Tier 1 · Week 1, before any feature work

  • 1
    Land first, alongside #2

    Founding / Staff Engineer

    Not "Backend Engineer #2" — this is the de facto tech lead day to day. Co-owns architecture with the founder, and is the primary reviewer of AI-generated code across the team. If you can only get one exceptional hire in month 1, this is it: everyone else's output quality runs through this person's review discipline.

  • 2
    Land first, alongside #1

    DevOps / Platform Engineer

    Required, not optional, in this scenario — the founder isn't hands-on, so nobody else naturally owns infra. Their first deliverables, before touching either product pillar: CI pipeline with mandatory checks, infrastructure as code, secrets management, structured logging and error tracking. Skipping this hire doesn't remove the work, it just means the work happens badly, later, under pressure.

Tier 2 · Weeks 2–4

  • 3
    Weeks 2–3

    QA / Verification Engineer

    Moved earlier than a typical plan would put it, on purpose: AI-generated code needs adversarial verification from the start, not a testing pass bolted on in month 4. Owns the test pyramid from day one and does deep, adversarial testing specifically on the two pillars and anything money-adjacent.

  • 4
    Weeks 2–3

    Second Mobile / Flutter Engineer

    Native UI work across two platforms is still real surface area regardless of AI assistance — visual polish, platform quirks, and App Store review issues aren't the kind of thing AI collapses the way it collapses backend boilerplate. Agent and Client parity is the part of scope that actually matters for the pilot.

  • 5
    Weeks 3–4

    Product / UX Designer

    The Transitional Document's authoring flow and the Merchant Integration onboarding flow are genuinely hard UX problems, not just forms. Worth a dedicated senior designer rather than engineers designing as they build, especially once there are two mobile engineers producing screens in parallel.

Tier 3 · Month 2

  • 6
    Month 2, deliberately not week 1

    Project Manager

    Earns its keep once there are 6+ builders across two flagship workstreams and three external dependency dates owed by other cofounders — real coordination complexity, not busywork. Brought in earlier than that, a PM mostly adds process overhead to two technical co-leads who are more efficient just talking to each other directly while the team is still small.

What I'd explicitly not hire yet

  • A dedicated "AI tooling" or prompt-engineering specialist — premature at 9 people; this is a practice (a written AI-usage policy, see Foundations), not a role, until the team is meaningfully larger
  • A second PM, a chief of staff, or any pure-coordination role beyond the one PM — a team this size drowns in process faster than it drowns in chaos
  • A dedicated security engineer as an FTE — contract a pen test before pilot launch instead; a full-time security hire is a Phase 2+ decision once there's real production traffic and real client data volume to justify it
  • Any compliance/legal hire on the build side — that function is owned by other cofounders in every scenario here on purpose (see Scope)
